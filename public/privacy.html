Privacy policy

Last updated: August 2026. Questions or requests: see the contact page.

The short version

Integral records behavior, not content. Test sessions capture clicks, mouse movement, scrolling, and timing. Anything a tester types is masked by default — masked values keep their length but not their characters. Integral adds no trackers, ads, or analytics scripts to the test-taking flow and sets no cookies on it, and taking a test never requires an account, an email address, or a browser permission prompt. The prototype itself is uploaded by the test creator and is served as they built it, so any scripts inside it are theirs, not ours.

If you take a test (tester)

  • What we collect: your interactions with the prototype (clicks, movement, scrolling, keystroke timing with masked content), task outcome, an optional difficulty rating and comment, an optional screener answer, and a one-way hash of your IP address and browser user-agent used solely to prevent duplicate responses.
  • What we don't collect: your name, email, precise location, or anything you type (unless the test creator explicitly un-masked a specific known-safe field, which is visible in the recording).
  • Consent: recording starts only after you accept the notice on the first screen.
  • Retention: session recordings are deleted automatically per the test owner's plan (14 days on the free tier, up to 1 year on paid), or earlier if the owner deletes the test. If a paid plan lapses, its recordings keep the paid retention window for a further 30 days before dropping to the free one.

If you create tests (account holder)

  • What we store: your GitHub profile basics (username, name, email, avatar), the artifacts you upload, your test configurations, and the sessions your tests collect.
  • Billing: handled entirely by Stripe. We never see or store card numbers; we store only your Stripe customer/subscription identifiers and plan status.
  • Deletion: deleting your account (dashboard → account → delete) immediately and permanently removes your profile and every test, uploaded artifact, and recorded session you own. If you belong to a workspace and are not its owner, the tests you created there belong to the workspace and stay with its owner when you leave; ask the owner to delete them, or get in touch. Deleting a workspace owner's account deletes the workspace and everything in it. Copies may persist in backups for up to 30 days after deletion before those backups age out. For a data export, get in touch.

Cookies

The test-taking flow sets no cookies at all. On the dashboard site we set two first-party cookies and no third-party ones: a signed session cookie that keeps you logged in, and an attribution cookie that records which channel you first arrived from (an ad tag, a referring site, or "direct") so we can tell which advertising is worth paying for. It expires after 30 days, is discarded once you sign up, holds no cross-site identifier, and nothing identifying is recorded for visitors who never create an account.

Site traffic

We count how many pages were viewed on the marketing site each day, so we know how many people saw it. The count is a running total per day and nothing else — no visitor identifier, no session, no page address, no IP address, no browser or device details are stored alongside it, and there is no third-party analytics script anywhere on the site. Two views on the same day cannot be told apart from one person reloading, which means these figures can never be linked back to you and there is nothing here to export or delete on request. Views are not counted while you are signed in.

Sub-processors

Data is stored on a single server in the United States. The services that touch data:

  • Hosting: Hetzner Online GmbH — server located in Hillsboro, Oregon, United States
  • Backups: S3-compatible object storage (database replicas and archives of uploaded artifacts and session recordings). Transfers are encrypted in transit; at rest they rely on the storage provider's own encryption rather than a key we hold.
  • Sign-in: GitHub, Inc. — accounts are created through GitHub OAuth, which is where your username, name, email, and avatar come from
  • Payments: Stripe, Inc.
  • Transactional email: Resend, Inc. (login and notification emails only)

Legal bases (GDPR)

Testers: consent (the recording notice). Account holders: contract (providing the service) and legitimate interest (abuse prevention, rate limiting). You may request access, correction, or erasure at any time; for tester sessions, contact the person who sent you the test link or the operator directly.

International transfers

Integral runs on a single server in the United States, and the sub-processors above are US companies. If you are in the EEA or UK, taking a test or holding an account means your data is transferred to and stored in the United States. Where those transfers need a safeguard, they rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses. If you would rather your data stay in the EEA, Integral is not the right tool for you today.
